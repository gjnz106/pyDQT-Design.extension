Fix Off Axis

by Dang Quoc Truong (DQT)

Warnings

Modify Toolset›Fix Off Axis

What it does

Turns elements that are a hair off the model axes back onto them - the cause of Revit's ... is slightly off axis and may cause inaccuracies warnings. Each element turns about its own midpoint onto the nearest axis, so it keeps its length and position; what is joined to it follows.

It handles Walls, Grids, Reference Planes, Model & Detail Lines, Room / Area / Space Separation Lines, Beams & Braces and the sketch lines of floors, roofs and ceilings in one window. It replaces the old Fix Element Off Axis pulldown (one button per category).

Workflow

  1. 1. Elements - tick the kinds to check.
  2. 2. Where - the whole model, what is visible in the active view, or the current selection.
  3. 3. Rules - keep Only elements in Revit's off-axis warnings ticked to fix exactly what Revit warns about. Untick it to find every element off by up to the angle limit (1° by default, at most 5° - beyond that a line is drawn at an angle on purpose). Tick Also snap to 45-degree diagonals for elements meant to sit at 45°.
  4. Scan - the list shows each element, how far it is off, how far its ends would move and whether Revit warns about it. Untick any you want left as they are.
  5. Fix Ticked - asks first, then turns them. A summary gives how many were fixed, any Revit refused, and Revit's off-axis warning count before and after. One Undo reverts the whole run.

Select in Revit closes the window and selects the ticked elements (all listed when none is ticked), so you can look at them first.

Which axis

In plan
walls, grids, beams and lines drawn in plan snap to model X / Y (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°). A sloped beam keeps its slope - only its direction in plan changes.
In elevation / section
a detail line, or a reference plane drawn in a section or elevation, snaps to that view's horizontal or vertical, and stays in its view plane.
Diagonals
with the 45-degree option, 45°, 135°, ... are targets too.

Sketch lines of floors, roofs and ceilings

A sketch line is one edge of a closed boundary, so it cannot turn on its own - its ends would come away from the next edges and Revit would not let the sketch finish. The tool does what Align + Trim/Extend to Corner do by hand in Edit Boundary: the edge goes onto the axis through its own midpoint and its two neighbouring edges are trimmed or extended to meet it, inside a sketch edit of that floor, roof (footprint) or ceiling.

In the list
named after its floor / roof / ceiling and its Id, with how far the corners move and how much the area changes - usually nothing, since the corners move along neighbours square to the edge
Where
Visible in the active view and Current selection go by the floor / roof / ceiling: select a floor to fix its sketch
Select in Revit
selects the floor / roof / ceiling (a sketch line can only be selected inside its sketch)
Checked
each edge is a sketch edit of its own; if Revit will not finish the sketch it is cancelled and named in the summary. Two off-axis edges side by side are fixed one after the other, the second from where the first left it

Left alone, to edit by hand:

Curved neighbour
an edge next to an arc
Neighbour along it
the next edge runs in the same direction (a split edge) - there is no corner to move
Too short
a neighbouring edge would become too short or turn back
Third line at a corner
a slope arrow or another line meets the edge there
Area over 0.1 %
the fix would change the area by more than 0.1 %
Shape edited
a floor or roof with Modify Sub Elements points
Other sketches
stairs, railings, openings, filled regions - listed only when Revit warns about them

Left alone (listed in orange)

Pinned
unpin it first if it should be fixed
In a group
edit the group and fix it there
Stacked wall part
fix the stacked wall itself
Multi-segment grid
a segment of a multi-segment grid
Another user's
borrowed by someone else in a workshared model
Over the limit
Revit warns about it, but it is off by more than the angle limit - raise the limit if it really should be straight

Good to know

Every element is turned in its own step and checked afterwards. One that Revit refuses (it cannot keep a join, a constraint gets in the way) is rolled back and named in the summary; the rest still go through.
Not handled: levels, other line-based families, and the sketches of stairs, railings, openings and filled regions. Their warnings stay in the "after" count.
New in v1.1 - sketch lines. The off-axis edges of floor, roof and ceiling boundaries, fixed the way Align + Trim/Extend fix them by hand.
Ends of long elements move furthest - a 10 m wall off by 0.05° moves each end about 4.4 mm. The list shows the movement for each element before you fix it.
Replaces Fix Element Off Axis. Its Fix Beam and Fix Line buttons measured the angle against the wrong axis and never turned anything (reporting "all aligned"), and Fix Ref Line showed the same name as Fix Line.
